# Common Workflows and End-to-End Tutorials

This guide turns FlowState features into realistic sequences. If the feature guides explain what each part of the interface does, this tutorial guide explains how to combine those parts to get real work done.

## Tutorial 1: Your first day in FlowState

If this is your first day using the app, follow this order.

### Step 1: Sign in or create your account

- open the FlowState site
- if you already have an account, sign in
- if you are creating an account, read the Privacy Policy and Terms of Use links
- complete registration and confirm that the form accepted your consent

### Step 2: Review your dashboard

- do not immediately click random pages
- scan for mentions, updates, and announcements
- decide what looks most important

### Step 3: Open your settings profile

- confirm your display name is correct
- add a short bio if useful for your team
- make sure other people will recognize you

### Step 4: Explore boards and threads carefully

- open the Boards page and see what boards you can access
- open the Threads page and notice the difference between DMs, channels, and replies
- do not rename or reorganize shared things unless you are meant to

### Step 5: Ask one simple question publicly if appropriate

If your team culture supports it, send a small introductory or clarifying message in the correct channel rather than disappearing into guesswork.

## Tutorial 2: Turning a request into a tracked task

Imagine someone asks for work in a message.

### Step 1: Read the request fully

Clarify:

- what is needed
- who owns it
- when it matters

### Step 2: Go to the correct board

Open the board where this type of work should live.

### Step 3: Create a new card

Use a specific title, such as:

- `Draft onboarding checklist for new external collaborator`

### Step 4: Fill in key details

Add:

- a description
- assignment
- due date if relevant
- helpful label

### Step 5: Return to the conversation

Reply with something like:

- `Tracked this on the Onboarding board and assigned it to myself for Friday review.`

This keeps planning and communication aligned.

## Tutorial 3: Running work through a board from start to finish

### Step 1: Start in the first appropriate list

Most work begins in something like:

- Backlog
- To Do
- Planned

### Step 2: Add enough detail early

Do not wait until the task is confusing before adding context.

### Step 3: Move the card when the status actually changes

Do not move a card to `Done` just because you started it. Let the board reflect reality.

### Step 4: Use comments for live updates

Add comments as decisions or progress happen.

### Step 5: Archive when the work no longer needs active visibility

Archival keeps the board cleaner without pretending the work never existed.

## Tutorial 4: Asking for review

### Step 1: Make sure the work object is clear

If the review is about a card, ensure the card contains the real context.

### Step 2: Mention the right person

Use a mention in the correct channel, reply, or card comment.

### Step 3: Ask for a specific kind of review

Examples:

- technical review
- copy review
- approval
- sign-off

### Step 4: Attach or link the needed material

Do not make the reviewer search blindly.

## Tutorial 5: Using focus mode without losing team awareness

### Step 1: Review dashboard signals first

Make sure nothing urgent needs attention.

### Step 2: Choose one real task

Open the board or conversation related to that task.

### Step 3: Start a focus session

Use the timer to create a defined work block.

### Step 4: Finish and reassess

When the timer ends:

- update the card or conversation if progress happened
- check whether anything important arrived while you were focused

## Tutorial 6: Reporting a bug properly

### Step 1: Go to Settings > Advanced

Open the bug report form.

### Step 2: Write a useful title

Example:

- `Card drag on Boards page reverts after drop`

### Step 3: Explain the steps

Include:

- where you were
- what you clicked
- what happened
- what you expected

### Step 4: Submit and watch the status

Check your recent reports list later to see whether the issue remains open, becomes triaged, or closes.

## Tutorial 7: Knowing when to use a DM versus a channel

Use a DM when:

- the conversation is private or one-to-one

Use a channel when:

- the answer should stay visible to a group

Use a reply when:

- the conversation is specifically about one message

If you are unsure, ask yourself:

- who actually needs this information later

## Tutorial 8: Recovering when you feel lost

If FlowState feels confusing in the moment:

1. stop clicking randomly
2. look at the page title
3. decide whether you are in Boards, Threads, Focus, or Settings
4. go back to the dashboard if needed
5. reopen the relevant guide in this documentation

That small reset often helps more than guessing.
