# Home Dashboard and Announcements

The home dashboard is the part of FlowState that answers a simple but important question:

- what should I pay attention to right now

If you skip the dashboard completely and jump at random into boards or threads, you may miss the fastest path to understanding what changed since you last visited.

## What the dashboard is for

The dashboard is not primarily where you edit large amounts of work. It is where you regain orientation.

It helps you:

- see recent changes
- notice mentions or items that need your attention
- see announcements meant for the wider workspace
- decide what to open next

## Why this page matters for beginners

Many people new to a collaborative web app feel lost because they do not know which page is “the right page.” The dashboard solves that problem by acting like a front desk for the rest of the app.

Instead of wondering:

- should I go to Boards
- should I go to Threads
- should I check Settings

the dashboard helps you decide by showing recent or relevant information first.

## What you may see on the dashboard

Depending on your current role, account state, and recent workspace activity, the dashboard may include:

- recent activity
- mentions or attention signals
- assigned or personally relevant work
- announcements
- summaries that help you decide what to open next
- focus entry points
- invite status panels for admins
- new joiner visibility
- activity highlight and team pulse areas

Not every user sees the exact same mix of information because access and activity differ.

## Activity and recency

An activity section helps answer:

- what changed while I was away

That is important because collaborative software often has more movement than one person can remember manually.

Useful activity examples:

- a card changed state
- someone replied in a thread you care about
- you were mentioned
- an announcement appeared

This keeps you from needing to manually open every possible page just to figure out what happened.

## Announcements

Announcements are usually more formal or more widely relevant than normal ongoing chat.

They are a good place for:

- team-wide reminders
- launch notices
- process changes
- upcoming deadlines
- operational notices

If a normal channel is like ongoing conversation, an announcement is more like a visible notice meant to be intentionally read.

## Announcement sending and audience controls

If your role allows it, the dashboard may also expose announcement creation tools. Those controls are not ordinary message posting tools. They are broader communication tools meant for workspace-wide or audience-targeted notices.

An authorized user may be able to:

- compose a subject
- write the announcement body
- send to everyone
- include or exclude particular roles
- include or exclude particular users

This means announcements can be broad or targeted, depending on the need. Use that carefully. A workspace announcement should feel intentional, not casual.

## Assigned work, created work, and summaries

The dashboard often distinguishes between:

- cards assigned to you
- cards created by you

That distinction matters because ownership and origin are different things. If you created a card for another person, it may matter for follow-up, but it is not the same as a card currently assigned to you.

Summary areas can also help you understand:

- recent completion
- recently archived or deleted work
- overall recent activity trends

These summaries are best for orientation, not detailed editing.

## Invites and admin-only controls

If you are an admin, the dashboard may include invite tools that let you:

- create a new invite
- copy the invite link
- share the invite link
- view pending, accepted, revoked, or expired invites

These are high-impact controls because they affect who can enter the workspace. They should be used carefully and intentionally.

## Team pulse and activity highlights

Some dashboard sections are less about “my next task” and more about “what is happening around the workspace.”

These areas can help you notice:

- recent collaboration
- visible momentum
- new joiners
- activity patterns

This is especially useful for leads, admins, and anyone coordinating group work.

## How to use the dashboard well

A strong daily pattern looks like this:

1. sign in
2. read the dashboard before jumping elsewhere
3. identify mentions, assignments, or announcements that matter now
4. open the specific board or thread that needs action

This helps you act intentionally instead of reacting to whichever page you happened to click first.

## Common mistakes on the dashboard

### Treating it like decoration

If you always ignore the dashboard, you give up one of the easiest ways to stay coordinated.

### Treating every item as equally urgent

A dashboard helps prioritize. It does not mean everything deserves attention in the same order.

### Confusing summaries with the work itself

The dashboard shows pointers and context. You will still usually open a board, thread, or other page to do the actual work.

### Using admin dashboard controls casually

If you are allowed to create invites or send announcements, remember that those actions affect other people immediately. Treat them like workspace actions, not just personal shortcuts.

## Good beginner habit

If you ever feel lost in FlowState:

- go back to the dashboard
- scan what has your attention
- then choose one next location on purpose

That reset is often enough to make the app feel understandable again.
